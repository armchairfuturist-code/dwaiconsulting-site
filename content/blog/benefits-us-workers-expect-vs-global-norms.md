If you're a global employer hiring in the United States for the first time, you might be surprised by how central health insurance is to your U.S. employees’ decision to accept or stay in a job. Compared to other countries with public healthcare systems, the U.S. workplace has uniquely high expectations when it comes to employer-provided benefits. Especially health coverage.

In this post, we’ll break down what U.S. employees typically expect, how those expectations differ from employees in other parts of the world, and how international employers can meet those expectations without overspending.

#### U.S. Workers Expect Health Coverage to Be the Norm

In many countries, access to healthcare is guaranteed regardless of employment. That’s not the case in the United States. Here, the majority of people under 65 rely on their employer for health insurance.

This leads to three key expectations:

1. Coverage should start quickly.
        Employees often expect eligibility on day one or within the first 30 days of employment.

2. The employer pays most of the cost.
        While employees typically contribute to premiums, they expect the employer to cover a significant portion—often 70% or more of the cost for the employee and at least some portion for dependents.

3. Benefits are part of compensation.
        U.S. workers view benefits as part of their total pay, not a bonus. Weak or confusing benefit options can lower the perceived value of the job.

#### Beyond Health: What Else U.S. Employees Want

While health insurance is the centerpiece, a strong benefits package often includes:

- Dental and vision insurance
- Life and disability coverage
- Retirement plans like a 401(k)
- Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) or Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs)
- Paid time off policies that are clearly defined

Each of these plays a role in creating a competitive total compensation package.
