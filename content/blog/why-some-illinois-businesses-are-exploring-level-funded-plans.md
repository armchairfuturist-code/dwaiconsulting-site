Healthcare costs continue to pressure small businesses across Illinois. For employers with fewer than 50 employees, traditional, fully insured health insurance plans are often viewed as the default option. However, some businesses are beginning to explore alternatives such as level-funded health plans.

Level-funded plans combine features of traditional insurance with elements commonly associated with self-funding. Instead of paying a fixed fully insured premium with little visibility into claims activity, employers make a predictable monthly payment that generally includes administrative costs, stop-loss protection, and estimated claims funding.

For certain groups, this structure may create additional flexibility and potential savings opportunities.

Businesses with younger employee populations, lower historical claims utilization, or strong participation levels are often the first to evaluate level-funded arrangements. Industries such as engineering firms, technology companies, and certain professional services firms sometimes fall into this category.

One reason employers explore level-funded plans is transparency. Traditional small-group plans can feel difficult to evaluate because employers rarely receive meaningful claims information or insight into cost drivers. Level-funded arrangements may provide more reporting and allow businesses to better understand how plan usage impacts overall costs.

Another factor is renewal volatility. While no health plan arrangement eliminates renewal increases entirely, some employers prefer having additional visibility into claims trends before renewal discussions occur.

Level-funded plans are not appropriate for every business. Employers should carefully review:

- participation requirements,
- employee demographics,
- cash flow considerations,
- provider networks,
- and stop-loss protection details.

Illinois employers should also understand that underwriting and eligibility requirements can differ significantly between carriers.

Businesses interested in reviewing plan structure, contribution strategies, or benchmark comparisons may want to begin with a broader evaluation of their current employee benefits approach before making major changes.
