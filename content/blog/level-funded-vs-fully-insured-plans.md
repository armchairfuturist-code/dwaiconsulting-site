Traditional fully insured health plans use fixed pricing structures that spread risk broadly across the market. While this approach provides predictability, it may not reflect the actual risk profile of every individual business. In this case, the term "fully-insured" means that all aspects of the insurance plan are being handled fully by one company (think, Anthem or United Healthcare)

Level-funded plans operate differently. They combine fixed monthly payments with self-funded components and stop-loss protection, allowing some businesses to potentially benefit from favorable claims experience and workforce demographics. In some cases, this can create opportunities for lower long-term costs while still maintaining strong provider network access and predictable monthly budgeting. The insurance component plans will be a mix of different providers. For example, one company might provide the insurance policies, another one provides the medical provider network and yet another might be handling the administrative duties.

The right approach depends on factors such as company size, employee demographics, participation levels, claims expectations, and long-term business goals. For many 10–50 employee businesses, evaluating both funding models can provide a better understanding of available options and long-term cost sustainability.
