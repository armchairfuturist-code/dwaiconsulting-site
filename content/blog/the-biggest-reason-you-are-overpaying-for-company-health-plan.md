Most insurance coverage is priced based on the risk you represent to the insurance company.

Have a lot of workplace accidents? You pay more for worker’s compensation coverage.

Have employees that are safe drivers? You pay less for vehicle coverage.

But health insurance, that is very different.

Are your employees healthy and rarely go to the doctor? You get to PAY THE SAME AMOUNT as a company of SOFAPOTAMUSES where everyone is 100 pounds overweight and has high blood pressure, coronary artery disease and diabetes.

#### WHY YOU ARE OVERPAYING AND WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT

You are overpaying because you are using small business ACA priced plans that determine premiums based on your geographic location instead of your group’s actual health needs.

Plans that price based on health needs fall under ERISA law and the most common option for small businesses is to use a “level-funded” health plan.

Level-funded health plans set premiums based on actual health needs as determined by confidential questionnaires filled out by your employees. If your group is healthy or does not use medical care often, rates can be much lower for the same comprehensive PPO plan you’re already using.

#### WHAT’S THE CATCH?

The catch is that it is different. The different components of a health insurance plan are managed by just one company when you use ACA plans. Level-funded plans are usually a combination of different providers.

#### DOES IT EVER GO WRONG?

Absolutely! Level-funded plans are priced based on the insurance company’s best guess of how much medical care your group will need for the year. The good news is that if they guess wrong and charge too much (meaning the plan was not used very much), then they will provide a refund of overpaid premiums to the business.

That’s right. THEY WILL PROVIDE A REFUND. And the average refund in 2022 was over $8,0001

If they underpriced it, you would NOT have to pay more. They will however raise your rates the next year but, you are not forced to renew the plan. If the rate increase is too steep, you can always switch back to an ACA plan and this is the strategy a lot of small businesses use to keep their costs as low as possible for as long as possible.
