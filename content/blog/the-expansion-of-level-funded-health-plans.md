For many years, self-funded health insurance was primarily reserved for large employers. Companies with hundreds or even thousands of employees had enough financial stability to take on the risk of paying employee medical claims directly. Smaller businesses generally stayed in the fully insured market, where the insurance carrier assumed the risk in exchange for fixed monthly premiums.

Over time, that began to change.

As healthcare costs continued to rise and underwriting technology improved, insurance companies and third-party administrators started developing new ways for smaller employers to participate in self-funded arrangements without taking on the same level of financial risk as a large corporation. This led to the growth of what is now commonly referred to as “level-funded” health insurance.

Today, level-funded plans are available to many businesses with as few as five enrolled employees. In some industries and demographic groups, these plans can provide lower costs, additional flexibility, and greater transparency than traditional small-group health insurance.

How Level Funding Works

A level-funded plan combines several components into one monthly payment:

- Administrative costs
- Expected medical claims
- Stop-loss insurance protection

In many cases, employers pay a consistent monthly amount similar to a traditional fully insured plan. However, if claims are lower than expected, some plans may provide surplus refunds at the end of the year.

At the same time, stop-loss protection is designed to limit catastrophic risk for the employer.

This structure helped bring self-funding “down market” from large corporations to smaller businesses.

Why the Market Has Expanded

Several trends have accelerated growth in the level-funded market over the past decade:

- Rising fully insured premiums
- Improved underwriting analytics
- Better stop-loss structures
- Increased interest from younger and healthier employer groups
- Advances in benefits administration technology
- Greater employer interest in plan customization

The market has also evolved beyond traditional insurance carriers. Today, employers can choose between large national carriers, modern technology-focused platforms, and newer alternative funding organizations.

Different Types of Level-Funded Providers

Not all level-funded carriers operate the same way. Some focus on broad provider networks and stability, while others emphasize technology, flexibility, or modern plan design.

Choosing the Right Fit

The “best” level-funded carrier often depends less on the carrier itself and more on the goals and demographics of the employer group.

Some employers prioritize: broad networks, stability and predictability while others focus on flexibility, employee experience, modern technology or alternative plan structures. The level-funded market has expanded rapidly over the past decade, and employers now have more options than ever before. As the industry continues evolving, we expect additional innovation, customization, and competition across the small-group benefits space.
