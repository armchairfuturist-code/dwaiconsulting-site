Offering health insurance is one of the most valuable benefits a small business can provide employees, helping attract and retain top talent while ensuring a healthier workforce. However, choosing the right health plan can be overwhelming, especially with the variety of options available.

If you're a small business owner looking to provide health benefits without breaking the bank, here’s a guide to the best types of health plans that balance cost, flexibility, and coverage.

#### 1. ICHRA (Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement)

Best for: Small businesses that want flexibility and cost control

An ICHRA allows businesses to reimburse employees for individual health insurance plans instead of offering a traditional group plan. This approach enables employees to choose coverage that suits their needs while allowing employers to set a budget without worrying about premium increases.

Why Small Businesses Like ICHRA:

✔ Cost Control: Employers set their reimbursement amount, making it easier to manage expenses.
✔ Employee Choice: Workers can pick the plan that works best for them.
✔ Avoids Group Plan Requirements: No need to meet participation or contribution requirements.
✔ Tax Benefits: Reimbursements are tax-free for employees and deductible for employers.

Ideal for: Small businesses with diverse employee needs or those struggling with high group plan costs.

#### 2. Level-Funded Health Plans

Best for: Businesses with healthy employees looking for savings

A level-funded plan is a type of self-funded health plan where employers pay a fixed monthly amount that covers claims, administrative fees, and stop-loss insurance (protection against high claims). If claims are lower than expected, the business gets a refund at the end of the year.

Why Small Businesses Like Level-Funded Plans:

✔ Potential Savings: If claims are lower than projected, you receive money back.
✔ More Affordable than ACA Small Group Plans: Companies with younger, healthier employees often save significantly.
✔ ERISA Advantage: These plans are regulated under ERISA rather than the ACA, offering more flexibility.

Ideal for: Companies with younger, healthier employees who want an alternative to traditional small-group plans.

#### 3. High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with an HRA

Best for: Businesses that want cost savings while providing richer benefits for employees

A High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with a Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA) is a strategic way for small businesses to save money while still offering strong benefits. Instead of just shifting costs to employees, the business provides an HRA to reimburse some or all of the out-of-pocket costs, making the plan feel more like a traditional health plan with lower employee costs.

Why Small Businesses Like HDHPs with HRAs:

✔ Lower Premiums: HDHPs cost less than traditional group health plans.
✔ Employer-Controlled Reimbursement: Employers decide how much to contribute to the HRA.
✔ Better Employee Benefits: Employees get reimbursement for deductibles and medical expenses, reducing their financial burden.
✔ Tax Benefits: Employer contributions are tax-deductible, and reimbursements are tax-free for employees.

Ideal for: Businesses that want lower premium costs but also want to offer competitive benefits without shifting too much cost to employees.

#### 4. Traditional Small Group Health Insurance (Fully Insured Plans)

Best for: Businesses that want a simple, predictable option

A fully insured plan is the most common health insurance option, where the business pays a set monthly premium to an insurance company. The insurer then takes on the risk of paying employees’ claims.

Why Small Businesses Like Fully Insured Plans:

✔ Predictable Costs: No unexpected claims expenses.
✔ Easy to Administer: The insurance company handles most of the work.
✔ No Financial Risk: Unlike level-funded plans, there’s no risk of losing money on claims.

Ideal for: Small businesses that prefer a straightforward, hands-off approach to employee health coverage.

#### 5. High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with a Health Savings Account (HSA)

Best for: Cost-conscious businesses and employees who want tax benefits

A High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) has lower monthly premiums but higher out-of-pocket costs. Employees can pair it with a Health Savings Account (HSA), where they save money tax-free for medical expenses.

Why Small Businesses Like HDHPs with HSAs:

✔ Lower Premiums: More affordable monthly costs compared to traditional plans.
✔ Tax Advantages: Employees and employers can contribute tax-free funds to the HSA.
✔ Employee Ownership: The HSA belongs to the employee, even if they leave the company.

Ideal for: Small businesses that want to lower costs while offering employees a way to save for medical expenses.

#### 6. Professional Employer Organization (PEO) Health Plans

Best for: Businesses that want to outsource HR functions and access large-group health insurance rates

A Professional Employer Organization (PEO) is a company that acts as a co-employer for small businesses, handling HR functions like payroll, compliance, and benefits administration. By joining a PEO, small businesses can access large-group health insurance rates, often at a lower cost than traditional small-group plans.

Why Small Businesses Like PEOs:

✔ Lower Premiums: PEOs pool multiple small businesses together to get better insurance rates.
✔ Less Administrative Work: The PEO handles compliance, payroll, and benefits, reducing the burden on business owners.
✔ More Comprehensive Benefits: Employees may get access to better coverage than they could with a small-group plan.

Ideal for: Small businesses that want to offer competitive benefits but don’t have the time or resources to manage HR and insurance administration.
