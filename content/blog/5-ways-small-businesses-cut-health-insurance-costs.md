## Popular health insurance alternatives for small businesses

## 1. Use Group Purchasing Power

Small businesses often face challenges in securing affordable insurance because insurers prefer larger groups with predictable risk profiles. But by coming together and leveraging group purchasing power, smaller businesses can negotiate better rates.

> "Group purchasing is a way for smaller businesses to use collective purchasing power to secure lower prices." - Horton Group [1]

Instead of tackling the insurance market solo, small businesses can pool their resources to create a larger risk group. This approach makes them more attractive to insurers, leading to reduced rates and improved contract terms.

### How Group Plans Benefit Small Businesses

When employees from multiple small businesses are grouped together, insurers can evaluate risk more evenly. This reduces the financial burden of unexpected, high-cost claims by spreading the risk across a larger pool. The result? More predictable claims costs and often substantial savings compared to purchasing coverage individually.

Group Purchasing Organizations (GPOs) play a key role in this process. They negotiate on behalf of their members, using the combined buying power of the group to secure discounted rates and pre-arranged contracts. This gives smaller businesses access to the kind of deals typically reserved for large corporations [2][3].

For health insurance, captives and PEOs are how small businesses realize savings.

## 2. Take Advantage of Tax Benefits and Credits

Offering health insurance to employees isn't just a way to attract and retain talent - it can also bring significant tax savings for your business. These tax benefits can substantially lower your actual costs, making health insurance more affordable than it might seem at first glance.

Understanding how to navigate these tax advantages is key to getting the most out of your investment in employee health benefits.

### Federal Tax Benefits for Employers

One of the biggest perks? Health insurance premiums are fully tax-deductible as a business expense. This means every dollar spent on premiums directly reduces your taxable income.

Here’s a quick example: If your business spends $50,000 on health insurance premiums and you’re taxed at a 25% rate, you’ll save $12,500 in taxes. That brings your real cost down to $37,500.

This deduction isn’t just for employees - it also applies to premiums paid for their families. And if you’re self-employed, you can deduct your health insurance premiums “above the line,” which lowers your adjusted gross income.

### Small Business Health Care Tax Credit

The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit offers even more savings. Unlike deductions, this credit directly reduces the amount of tax you owe, dollar for dollar.

To qualify, your business must:

- Employ fewer than 25 full-time employees
- Pay average wages below $64,000
- Cover at least 50% of employee premiums
- Purchase a plan through the Small Business Health Options Program (SHOP) marketplace

If you meet these requirements, the credit can cover up to 50% of premiums paid for small businesses and 35% for tax-exempt organizations. The credit gradually decreases as your employee count or average wages approach the upper limits. However, businesses with 10 or fewer employees earning average wages of $29,000 or less may qualify for the full credit percentage. Even a partial credit can lead to meaningful savings.

## 3. Focus on Preventive Care and Wellness Programs

Taking care of employee health isn’t just good for morale - it’s a smart way to save on costs. Preventive care and wellness initiatives can help employees stay healthier, which, in turn, reduces medical claims and keeps insurance premium rates more consistent over time. This forward-thinking approach ties in perfectly with other cost-saving strategies discussed throughout this guide.

By tackling health issues early - through regular checkups, screenings, and vaccinations - you can avoid expensive treatments down the road. Insurers often reward lower claim rates with more predictable premiums, making this a win-win for both employers and employees.

Thanks to provisions in the Affordable Care Act, many health insurance plans now cover preventive services without requiring a deductible. This means employees can access flu shots, colonoscopies, blood pressure checks, and other essential screenings without paying out of pocket. Beyond cutting direct medical costs, preventive care also helps reduce absenteeism, keeping your workforce both healthy and productive.

### Examples of Low-Cost Wellness Programs

Wellness programs are another practical way to promote health and save money - no massive budget required. These initiatives not only complement preventive care but can also make a noticeable difference in employee well-being and insurance costs.

Simple ideas like fitness challenges or step competitions can motivate employees to stay active. Smoking cessation programs can tackle tobacco-related health concerns, while mental health resources, such as employee assistance programs, offer affordable ways to address workplace stress.

You can also bring health services directly to your team. For example, consider offering on-site screenings or hosting nutrition workshops. Stocking healthier snack options in the break room is another small but effective step toward promoting better eating habits.

Start with manageable, easy-to-track programs and build from there as participation grows. Monitoring engagement and overall health trends can give you a clear picture of how these efforts are impacting both employee wellness and insurance costs. And when leadership gets involved, it often inspires a broader shift toward prioritizing health, creating a ripple effect that benefits the entire organization.

## 4. Use High Deductible Health Plans (HDHPs) with Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)

Pairing High Deductible Health Plans (HDHPs) with Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) can lower healthcare costs while giving employees greater control over their medical expenses. This setup not only reduces monthly premiums but also offers long-term financial advantages that can positively impact your business's bottom line.

The primary appeal of HDHPs is their lower monthly premiums compared to traditional health plans. For small businesses working within tight budgets, these reduced premiums can free up funds for other essential needs. When combined with an HSA, this option becomes more than just a cost-cutting measure - it turns into a financial tool that benefits both employers and employees.

### What Are HDHPs and HSAs?

An HDHP is a health insurance plan with a higher deductible than traditional plans. According to the IRS, for 2025, an HDHP must have a minimum deductible of $1,650 for individual coverage and $3,300 for family coverage, with maximum out-of-pocket limits of $8,300 for individuals and $16,600 for families.

HSAs complement HDHPs by serving as specialized savings accounts for medical expenses. Both employers and employees can contribute to these accounts, and the funds roll over indefinitely - they never expire. Unlike flexible spending accounts, which follow a "use it or lose it" rule, HSAs belong to the employee, even if they leave the company.

For employers, the premium savings from HDHPs can amount to thousands of dollars annually per employee. Employees, on the other hand, benefit from having an account that not only covers medical expenses but also grows over time. This combination encourages smarter healthcare spending, as employees are more likely to compare prices and assess the necessity of certain procedures when using their own money.

Beyond immediate savings, this approach also provides substantial tax advantages, as outlined below.

### Tax Benefits of HSAs

HSAs offer a rare triple tax advantage, making them a highly attractive option for both employees and employers.

- Tax-deductible contributions: Employer contributions to HSAs are fully deductible as a business expense. Employees also benefit by reducing their taxable income dollar-for-dollar with their contributions. In 2025, contribution limits are set at $4,300 for individual coverage and $8,550 for family coverage, with an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution for employees aged 55 and older.
- Tax-free growth: Unlike traditional savings accounts, HSA funds can be invested in options like mutual funds and stocks. Any earnings, dividends, or capital gains grow tax-free, turning the HSA into a long-term savings tool.
- Tax-free withdrawals for medical expenses: Employees can use HSA funds for a wide range of qualified medical expenses - doctor visits, prescriptions, dental care, and even some alternative treatments - without paying taxes on the withdrawals. This also applies to items not typically covered by insurance, such as certain over-the-counter medications and medical equipment.

The tax savings add up quickly. For example, an employee in the 22% tax bracket who maximizes their HSA contribution saves $946 annually in federal taxes for individual coverage or $1,881 for family coverage. When you include state taxes and potential FICA savings, the total tax benefit for families often exceeds $2,000 per year.

For employers, contributing to HSAs offers the same tax advantages as traditional health insurance premiums but often at a much lower overall cost. Many small businesses find they can contribute between $1,000 and $2,000 per employee while still spending less than they would on a traditional low-deductible plan.

HSAs are especially appealing to younger, healthier employees due to their long-term savings potential. After age 65, HSA funds can be used for any purpose, functioning like a traditional IRA, though non-medical withdrawals are subject to income tax. This flexibility, combined with tax-free medical withdrawals, makes HSAs a smart choice for building financial security over a lifetime.

## 5. Customize Plan Design and Payment Strategies

To make the most of your health insurance investment, it's crucial to shape your offerings around your employees' specific needs. By combining group and tax savings with a carefully tailored plan design, you can take cost control to the next level.

Start by getting a clear picture of your workforce's healthcare habits and financial priorities. For instance, a tech startup with younger employees will likely have very different needs compared to a manufacturing company with an older workforce. This kind of targeted planning ensures you're directing resources toward benefits employees will actually use, avoiding unnecessary spending on services that go untouched.

### Match Plan Options to Employee Needs

Your workforce isn't one-size-fits-all, and your health plans shouldn't be either. By analyzing demographics and healthcare usage, you can tailor options to better serve your team.

For example:

- Younger employees may prefer high-deductible plans that keep premiums low but cover unexpected expenses.
- Older employees or those with chronic conditions might benefit from plans with lower deductibles and broader provider networks.

Additionally, consider local provider availability when designing your network options. Narrow network plans, which focus on a smaller range of providers, can often reduce premiums - especially in areas with quality hospital systems.

Offering multiple plan options is another way to meet diverse needs. Include choices like cost-conscious plans with higher deductibles alongside more comprehensive plans with lower out-of-pocket costs. This flexibility allows employees to pick coverage that fits their personal circumstances.

### Split Premium Costs Between Employer and Employee

Sharing premium costs is a smart way to balance affordability and manage expenses. For instance, you might cover a larger portion of individual premiums while adjusting family contributions to reflect the higher costs of covering dependents. This approach keeps benefits affordable for employees while maintaining predictable costs for your business.

You can also explore tiered, defined, or salary-based contribution models to align cost-sharing with your broader financial strategy. These methods complement other savings tactics and give you more control over benefit expenses.

### Compare Costs Across Plan Types

A thorough cost comparison is essential when choosing plan options. Look at factors like monthly premiums, deductibles, cost-sharing structures, and out-of-pocket maximums to find the right balance between affordability and comprehensive coverage.

For example:

- High-deductible health plans (HDHPs) paired with health savings accounts (HSAs) often come with lower premiums but require employees to cover more upfront costs.
- Preferred provider organization (PPO) plans may have higher premiums but offer more predictable expenses, which some employees may prefer.

By evaluating these variables, you can select plans that align with both your budget and your employees' needs.

## FAQs

### How can small businesses use group purchasing power to lower health insurance costs?

Small businesses have a smart way to cut down on health insurance costs: group purchasing power. By joining purchasing groups (PEOs, captives) or associations, multiple businesses can pool their resources to negotiate better deals with insurance providers. This collective approach often leads to lower premiums and improved coverage options.

Another effective tactic is joining associations that have a multiple employer welfare arrangement (MEWA). These can make it easier to access affordable plans that still offer solid coverage. These strategies not only help businesses save money but also ensure employees receive quality benefits, which can go a long way in boosting satisfaction and retention.

### What are the eligibility requirements for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit, and how can small businesses take full advantage of it?

To qualify for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit, your business needs to meet a few specific requirements:

- Employ fewer than 25 full-time workers
- Pay an average annual wage of $56,000 or less per employee
- Cover at least 50% of health insurance premiums for full-time employees
- Provide health coverage through the Small Business Health Options Program (SHOP)

Meeting these criteria can make a big difference for your business. The tax credit can cover up to 50% of your premium costs, helping you save money while offering meaningful benefits to your team. It's not just about cutting expenses - it’s also a smart way to enhance employee satisfaction.

### What are the benefits of pairing a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with a Health Savings Account (HSA) for small businesses and their employees?

Pairing a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) with a Health Savings Account (HSA) can be a smart move for small businesses and their employees. For employers, HDHPs often come with lower premiums, which can help cut down overall healthcare expenses. Plus, any contributions made to employees' HSAs are tax-deductible, adding another layer of financial benefit.

For employees, the perks are just as appealing. They can make tax-free contributions to their HSA, use those funds for qualified medical expenses, and even save for future healthcare needs. The best part? HSA funds grow tax-free and stay with employees, even if they switch jobs, offering long-term financial peace of mind. This setup not only promotes more thoughtful healthcare spending but also helps both employers and employees keep costs under control.
