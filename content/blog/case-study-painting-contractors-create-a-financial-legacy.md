#### A Beginner’s Experience with Tax Reduction, Wealth Accumulation and Asset Protection

Owning a business is, financially speaking, the riskiest way to make a living. More business owners fail than succeed and if anything goes wrong, all your income and assets are at risk because you have everything in this one basket.

Properly structuring your compensation can significantly impact your tax liability and long-term financial growth. In this case study, we will explore the impacts on the two owners of an Arizona-based S-Corp earning $400,000 in profits each – comparing tax liabilities and showing how strategic planning can optimize financial outcomes.

Let’s look at the current compensation structure for each owner: 60% Wages, 40% Distribution

Compensation: Wages: $240,000 | Distributions: $160,000

Total Taxes: $128,864 (Includes federal, state, and FICA)

Remaining Cash and Investments: $271,136

#### Step One – Tax Savings and Wealth Accumulation

A slight increase to wages allows the owners to maximize pre-tax contributions to a SEP IRA which provides tax savings now for the business owners and allows tax-deferred growth while also being a powerful tool for wealth accumulation. The SEP contribution is subtracted from wages, lowering the overall tax obligation.

New Compensation Structure for each owner: $265K Wages, $135K Distribution, $69K SEP IRA Contribution

Compensation: Wages: $265,000 | Distributions: $135,000 | SEP IRA: $69,000

Total Taxes: $112,389 (Includes federal, state and FICA)

Cash and Investments: $287,611

This is over $16,000 in tax savings and additional wealth – for each of them – every year.

Assuming the S-Corp owners contribute this $69,000 annually for 10 years with the standard average market return of 10%, their anticipated value is $1,209,651.

After ten years, if the S-Corp owners decide to stop investing for some reason, the accumulated returns would continue to grow and by age 60, the accounts would each be worth almost $9,000.000,00.

However, there are no guarantees regarding market returns and there will be tax consequences once the owners start withdrawing their money.

#### Step Two – More Tax Savings, Wealth Accumulation and Asset Protection

To mitigate the risk associated with investing and provide increased asset protection, a $4,500,000 Indexed Universal Life (IUL) policy is added. It is funded over ten years and provides tax-advantaged growth and tax-free access to funds later.

With an IUL, policyholders benefit from principal protection (never losing money due toa market downturns) while still participating in market-linked growth. Though also not guaranteed, the policy’s value could realistically grow to more than $10,000,000.00.

They can also access this money tax-free making the IUL a great way to counter the potential future tax liabilities from the SEP or, it could be designed as a tax-free pension.

Now, each owner has created a minimum legacy for their families of nearly $20,000,000.00.

#### Step Three – Buy/Sell Agreement

Lastly, there needs to be protection for each owner’s business interest in the event tragedy strikes. Currently the company is valued at $1,000,000.00.

A buy/sell agreement is put in place backed by a term life insurance policy. This is used to fund the purchase of the business, so no one has to worry about securing loans or selling personal assets to buy out the other owner’s estate. The insurance does this for us and we will have to update the policy every few years to reflect the increase in the value of the business as it grows.

#### What if My Company Is Not This Big?

Size truly does not matter. Any business owner can use these strategies. The amounts, investments and insurance products are simply scaled to fit the situation. In fact, it is better to start earlier because your money has longer to grow thanks to compound interest.

In the example above, had the owners started five years earlier with smaller amounts, their financial legacies would be estimated nearly 30% higher than their current projection.

#### Conclusion: The First Steps Toward A Financial Legacy

Though owning a business is financially risky, properly structuring compensation and sound planning can mitigate the risks.

S-Corp owners have a lot of different options available to them and whether the goal is reducing current tax burdens, accumulating long-term wealth, or securing assets, there is a strategy that can ensure financial stability in both the short and long term.
