Life insurance is one of the most common, yet least understood, benefits offered by employers. While many employees appreciate that coverage exists, few fully understand their options or how to choose the right one. As an employer, demystifying life insurance can boost perceived value and employee peace of mind.

#### Group Term Life Insurance

This is the most common employer-provided benefit. It typically offers a flat amount or a multiple of salary (e.g., 1x or 2x annual pay) and is often provided at no cost to employees. However, it usually ends when the employee leaves the company. As of the date of this writing, many small employers limit the coverage they pay on behalf of employees to $50,000 because these premiums are excluded from an employee’s federal taxable income. Premiums for coverage exceeding $50,000 are considered taxable income and are subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

#### Voluntary Life Insurance

Because $50,000 worth of coverage may not be enough, employers often provide employees the option to buy additional coverage on their own. This may be portable and can sometimes cover spouses and children. Rates are group-based and depending on the amount of coverage, can be written as guaranteed issue (without medical exams).

#### Permanent Life Insurance as a Benefit

Less common but growing in use among executive teams, permanent policies like whole or universal life can accumulate cash value and be used in executive bonus or split-dollar arrangements. These are valuable tools for retention, wealth planning, and offering long-term incentives that align executive interests with the company’s success.

#### Convertible Options

Good plans include a convertibility feature, allowing employees to switch to an individual policy when they leave the company which is an important consideration for employees with health concerns.

#### Employee Education and Access to Expertise is Key

No matter what coverage is offered, it's important to educate employees. Host workshops, provide benefit statements, and offer one-on-one guidance with experts so that employees understand:

How much coverage they need

How their age and health affect cost

The difference between term and permanent insurance

Access to experts that can help them with additional planning needs outside of the company offerings

#### Final Thoughts

Life insurance is more than a checkbox benefit. With the right mix of coverage options and education, it becomes a meaningful part of your employees' financial safety net — and a differentiator in your overall benefits strategy.
