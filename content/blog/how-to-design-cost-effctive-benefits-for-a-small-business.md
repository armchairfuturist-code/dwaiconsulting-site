In today’s competitive hiring environment, employee benefits are no longer optional, they are a necessity. But for small business owners, offering a robust benefits package can feel out of reach. The good news? You don’t need a Fortune 500 budget to offer meaningful perks that attract and retain top talent. With the right strategy, you can design a cost-effective benefits package that delivers real value to both your team and your bottom line.

#### Start With What Matters Most to Your Employees

Every workforce is different so don't just assume everyone wants a health plan. Many times your employees already have that covered. Take time to understand what your team actually values. Older workers might prioritize health coverage and younger employees student loan assistance or paid time off.

Tip: Send out an anonymous survey asking employees to rank benefits by importance. You may be surprised by the results and it could save you money by focusing on what really matters.

#### Explore Ancillary Benefits

Ancillary benefits, such as dental, vision, life insurance, and disability coverage, are low-cost to the employer and can provide enourmous value for employees. You can add multiple benefits at a fraction of the cost of a health plan and it's a great way to get started.

#### Explore Health Plan Alternatives

If traditional group health insurance is out of your budget, consider options like:

- Level-Funded Health Plans – Hybrid plans with predictable costs and the potential for year-end refunds.
- ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA) – Employers reimburse employees for individual health insurance plans.
- Health Sharing Plans – Not insurance, but may appeal to some employees as a cost-saving alternative.

Each option has trade-offs, so working with a benefits advisor can help ensure compliance and alignment with your workforce.

#### Offer Flexible Work Arrangements

Flexibility is one of the most cost-effective benefits you can offer. Remote work, flexible hours, and compressed workweeks cost little or nothing but greatly enhance employee satisfaction and retention.

#### Provide Financial Wellness Tools

Financial stress affects productivity. Even basic offerings like access to a financial coach, budgeting tools, or workshops can make a big difference. Some platforms offer these services at low or no cost through retirement plan vendors or EAPs. These are often included as free value-adds with some life insurance, health or disability insurance offerings depending on the carrier

#### Educate and Communicate

A great benefits package is only effective if your employees understand it. Provide clear explanations, hold Q&A sessions, and use visual guides to help them take full advantage of what you offer. Better engagement equals better ROI. You should also consider providing a total compensation statement to your employees so they can see the tangible value they receive.

#### Don't Forget Yourself

Most small business owners think of themselves last/ They are so focused on their clients, employees and the growth of their business that they miss out on simple ways to improve the personal value they receive through the business. Adding a simple disability policy, life insurance and a basic retirement plan are very low cost ways to not only improve your hiring and retention efforts but, they are a great way to add expense deductions that lower your taxes while providing more value for yourself.

#### Final Thoughts

You don’t need to match the benefits of a large corporation to stay competitive. By focusing on what your employees value, using smart plan designs, and emphasizing education, you can build a cost-effective benefits package that supports your team and strengthens your business.
