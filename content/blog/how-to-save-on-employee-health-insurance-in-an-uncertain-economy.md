#### Don’t Cut Quality - Cut Waste

Let’s face it, when the economy gets shaky, business owners feel it first. Margins shrink. Growth slows. And those “fixed” costs like health insurance? They start to look a lot less fixed once you see what’s possible.

The truth is, many small businesses are overpaying for health insurance, especially under ACA small group plans. And in a tough economy, that kind of waste isn’t just annoying, it’s a threat to your bottom line.

Here’s how you can start saving money without slashing benefits or disappointing your team.

#### 1. Eliminate the Lack of Transparency

If you have fewer than 50 employees, chances are your health plan is working against you in two ways.

First, you might be in a high-risk pool. Even if your team isn’t.

ACA small group rules don’t allow real underwriting. That means your pricing is based on the general market, not your specific employees. So even if your team is young, healthy, and low-risk, you’re subsidizing the other businesses that are incurring higher healthcare costs.

Second, you can’t see how your plan is actually being used.

Small group fully insured plans usually don’t provide claims data, not even to your broker. So, you might be paying for “ghost benefits” no one uses (like acupuncture or rich out-of-network coverage) without even realizing it.

Bottom line: You’re likely overpaying both for who you are and what you’re buying.

What to do:

- Consider level-funded plans which allow for more customized options and pricing.
- Level-funded plans will provide a quote based on your actual employee demographics and medical needs. You might be shocked at how much you can save.
- They also provide claims information that will guide future plan designs.

#### 2. Stop Over-Insuring Your Team

This one’s a silent killer. Most employees don’t hit their deductible, and many don’t even use the plan beyond preventive care. Yet companies are paying for low-deductible, gold-tier PPO plans that go mostly untouched.

Why this matters:

- You’re paying high premiums for benefits your team isn’t even using.
- Employees might feel like they’re getting better coverage, but the math doesn’t support the cost.

What to try instead:

- Offer a higher-deductible plan (or an HSA-eligible option) and fund part of the deductible with an HRA or health savings account.
- Pair a leaner medical plan with voluntary benefits (like accident, critical illness, or dental/vision) so employees still feel protected without you footing the full bill.

#### 3. Switch to an ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA)

An ICHRA lets you give employees tax-free money to buy their own health insurance. You stay out of the insurance business. They get more choice. And you can cap your cost predictably.

Why now:

- The individual insurance market is often cheaper, especially in competitive states.
- You control the budget. Period.
- No more renewals that give you sticker shock.

#### 4. Rethink Your Network: PPO vs. HMO

A lot of businesses pay extra for PPO networks because they sound more flexible. But most employees still stay in-network for cost reasons and many never use the “extra” access they’re paying for.

Ask yourself:

- How many employees are actually seeing out-of-network providers?
- Would a more structured network (like an HMO or EPO) work just as well or even better?

What to try:

- Run a network analysis: See where your employees actually get care and whether they’d be just as well-served by a smaller network.
- Consider HMO options that include major hospitals or health systems in your area. The premium savings can be significant.

#### 5. Use a Benefits Strategy That Supports Recruitment & Retention

Cutting benefits sounds tempting, but it can backfire fast, especially in industries with high turnover or hiring challenges. Instead, restructure benefits to match what employees actually value.

Example:

- Offer a lean core medical plan + optional extras through voluntary benefits.
- Use a health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) to give flexibility without giving up control.
- Consider adding or beefing up retirement plans and life insurance as they are WAY less expensive than a health plan and provide lasting, tangible monetary value for employees.

Final Thoughts

Tightening your belt doesn’t mean short-changing your employees or settling for worse care. It means being smarter about how you spend.

In a shaky economy, savings = strength. If you haven’t reviewed your health insurance strategy in the last 12 months, now’s the time. A few smart changes can free up thousands in cash flow without compromising on care.
