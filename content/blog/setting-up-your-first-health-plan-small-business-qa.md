Thinking about offering health insurance for the first time? You’re not alone. Many small business owners reach the point where benefits aren’t just “nice to have”, they’re a must for attracting and keeping great employees. But we know the process can feel confusing. Below are answers to the most common questions small business owners ask.

### Q: Why should I even offer health insurance? Isn’t it too expensive?

A: Offering health benefits is one of the best ways to stand out as an employer. Most employees consider health insurance their top requested benefit. And here’s something many business owners don’t realize: health insurance can actually be less expensive than giving the same amount in wages.

Here’s why: every dollar of wages is subject to payroll taxes (FICA, unemployment insurance, etc.), but money you put toward health insurance premiums is not. That means you save on payroll taxes, and your employees save too, since they’re getting health coverage with pre-tax dollars instead of paying out of pocket.

### Q: How much does it usually cost?

A: Costs depend on your workforce and the plan you choose, but on average, small businesses spend around $6,500 - $7,000 per employee per year. That number can vary wildly depending on the type of plan you select and the age of your employees. You’ll decide how much the company contributes vs. what employees pay. The minimum employer contribution is 50% of the premium but most contribute around 80% - 85%.

### Q: What types of plans are available to a small business like mine?

A: You have three main options:

- Fully insured plans: The simplest option with predictable monthly premiums, less paperwork.
- Level-funded plans: Good for younger/healthier workforces, often lower cost, with potential refunds if claims are low.
- ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA): You set a budget, and employees buy their own coverage. Very flexible.

### Q: What do I need to get started?

A: The basics are:

- Your business tax ID, SIC code and number of employees.
- An employee “census” (ages, zip codes, genders and dependent info, but no health details).
- Your budget or how much you’re willing to contribute per employee or per plan.

Once you have that, a broker or consultant can shop plans and present you with options.

#### Q: How long does it take to set up a plan?

A: Give it at least 30 days if you want it to be a smooth process. You'll need to submit an application and a lot of supporting documentation to the insurance carrier so they can verify that you are a legitimate business with actual employees. This can include but is not limited to: Latest wage and tax filing, SS4 (EIN) document, business license, articles of incorporation, individual applications / waivers for coverage, company application and more.

### Q: Do I have to offer the same plan to everyone?

A: Not necessarily. Some businesses offer one plan, others give employees a choice of two or three (for example, a lower-cost high-deductible plan and a richer PPO). The right number depends on your budget and how much choice you want to provide.

### Q: How do employees sign up?

A: Many small businesses still use paper enrollment forms submitted to the carrier although most carriers and brokers provide an online enrollment portal. Employees can compare plans side by side, see what their paycheck contributions would be, and enroll electronically. Clear communication is key here. Make sure employees understand the trade-offs between cost and coverage.

### Q: Are there compliance requirements I need to worry about?

A: Yes. If you have employees, you know there are hundreds of different federal and state employment laws you have to be careful not to violate. For health benefits, the two most people are concerned with are based on your size:

- Under 50 employees: Generally, you’re not required to offer coverage, but you must follow basic rules under ERISA.
- 50+ employees: You’ll need to meet ACA requirements.
  There may also be small annual fees (like the PCORI fee) and state-specific requirements, which your broker can help with.

### Q: Bottom line — is it worth it?

A: Absolutely. Beyond the payroll tax savings, offering health benefits makes you more competitive in hiring, improves retention, and shows employees you’re invested in them. For many small businesses, this step marks the transition from being just a workplace to being an employer of choice.

👉 Next step: Gather your employee census and set a budget. With those two pieces, you’ll have everything you need to start shopping for plans.
