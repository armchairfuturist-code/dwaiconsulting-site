For international companies employing staff in the United States offering employee benefits (health insurance and retirement plans) isn’t just a way to stay competitive, it can also be a smart financial decision. U.S. tax law provides significant advantages for employers who offer health benefits and retirement plans, helping reduce overall payroll tax liability while improving employee satisfaction.

In this post, we’ll break down the key tax benefits for non-U.S. employers offering employee benefits.

#### Why Tax Treatment Matters in the U.S.

Unlike many countries with government-funded healthcare and pension systems, the U.S. encourages employer-sponsored insurance and retirement planning through the tax code. Health benefits and retirement contributions, when properly structured, are excluded from both employer payroll taxes and employee income taxes, making them one of the most tax-efficient forms of compensation.

#### Employer Tax Advantages

1.	Payroll Tax Savings

Every dollar your company pays employees in wages is subject to various payroll taxes and expenses for the business. Employer contributions to health insurance and retirement however are excluded from federal income tax, state income tax, social security, medicare, state unemployment insurance and federal unemployment taxes (FUTA). This can lower your total employment costs significantly per dollar contributed and provide employees with greater overall compensation.

2.	Deductible Business Expense

Health insurance premiums, retirement plan administration costs and retirement plan contributions paid by the company are tax-deductible as an ordinary business expense - reducing your corporate tax liability while retirement plans also qualify the business for additional tax credits under the SECURE Act.

3.	Reduced Workers’ Compensation Premiums

Though not a tax it can be thought of one since most states mandate this coverage for workplace incidents. In many cases, we see significantly fewer workers compensation claims in companies with comprehensive health coverage. It's assumed that since employees have health coverage, they are less likely to fake on the job injuries in order to get medical coverage. Also, health insurance premiums and retirement contributions are not included in wage calculations so they are not included in the calcuations for workers compensation rates.

#### Employee Tax Advantages

1.	Tax-Free Benefit

Employees do not pay federal income, state income nor payroll taxes (listed above) on employer-paid health premiums and retirement contributions. This increases their overall compensation without increasing taxable income.

2.	HSA & FSA Accounts

When paired with the right plan, employees can set aside pre-tax dollars for medical expenses which gives them more take-home value with less cost to the employer.

#### Considerations for Global Employers

•	Entity Structure Matters: To take full advantage of U.S. tax benefits, your company may need to establish a U.S. entity.

•	Document Compliance: ERISA, ACA, IRS and state regulations require plan documentation and employee notices to preserve tax-favored treatment.

•	Avoid Misclassification: Health plans should not be treated as cash equivalents.

•	Budget Expectations - Company does NOT Pay For 100%: Company health plan contributions rarely cover 100% of the total cost. 80% to 85% for employee only coverage and about 70% for family coverage is the average as of the writing of this article. Retirement plan contributions hover around 4% to 5% of wages.

•	Think in Terms of Total Compensation: U.S. employees think of thier compensation as a combination of wages and other employee benefits. Since the US tax code incentivizes businesses to provide benefits with expense deductions and tax credits, shifting a percentage of the overall compensation budget to benefits can provide more value for employees at a lower net cost to the business.
