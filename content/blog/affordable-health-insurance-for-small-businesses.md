Finding the Right Balance Between Cost and Coverage

Small businesses face an uphill battle when it comes to providing health insurance. Rising premiums, complex regulations, and a lack of affordable options leave many owners frustrated. But what if the problem isn’t just the cost, but how small businesses approach health benefits? Instead of sticking to the traditional playbook, it’s time to rethink coverage strategies to find solutions that are both affordable and effective.

Here’s how to make smarter choices when offering health benefits to your employees:

\1. Stop Over-Insuring

Many small businesses buy coverage with low deductibles and rich benefits, thinking they’re doing right by their employees. But these plans come at a high price—often unnecessarily. Employees rarely use every benefit, meaning your business is paying for coverage that goes underutilized. Instead, focus on essential benefits that employees value most and structure plans in a way that reduces waste.

\2. Stop Hating HMOs for No Reason

Many business owners avoid HMOs (Health Maintenance Organizations) because of outdated assumptions about poor networks or limited doctor choices. In reality, today’s HMOs offer robust networks and cost much less than traditional PPOs. By considering an HMO, you can reduce premiums without sacrificing quality care.

\3. Look at ERISA Strategies

If your business has the right demographics—such as a younger and healthier workforce—you may be overpaying under ACA small group rules. ERISA-based strategies, like level-funded plans, allow businesses to lower costs because they can be customized and are priced more accurately to reflect the lower risk the group presents to the insurance company. These plans also offer the potential for refunds on unused claims dollars, something fully insured plans can’t provide.

\4. Stop Focusing Only on Deductibles and Max Out-of-Pocket Costs

Most small businesses shop for health insurance based on two numbers: the deductible and max out-of-pocket. But the real cost driver isn’t just what employees pay when they get sick—it’s what your business pays in premiums every month. By looking deeper into cost structures, such as provider reimbursements and prescription costs, you can find ways to reduce premiums while still offering great coverage.

\5. Maybe Cancel the Plan and Set Up an ICHRA

For some businesses, the best move is to ditch traditional group insurance altogether and switch to an Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement (ICHRA). ICHRAs let employees pick their own health plans, while employers set a budget that fits their needs. This approach gives employees more choices and can reduce employer costs.

\6. If You’re a New Business, Consider Other High-Impact Benefits

If your business is just starting out, traditional health insurance may not be the best first investment. Other benefits—like company-paid life insurance, disability coverage, or a well-structured retirement plan—can provide meaningful financial security for employees at a fraction of the cost of a health plan. These benefits can help you attract and retain talent without breaking the bank.

The Bottom Line

Affordable health insurance isn’t about finding the cheapest plan—it’s about structuring benefits strategically. Whether it’s adjusting coverage levels, considering an HMO, exploring ERISA strategies, or even replacing traditional insurance with an ICHRA, small businesses have more options than they realize. The key is to think beyond the surface and focus on what really drives cost and value.
